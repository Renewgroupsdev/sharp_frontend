# Sample content guide: course, assessment, assignment and quiz

Step-by-step instructions for creating a course and the three kinds of graded work in SHARP, with sample values you can type in as they are. Sign in as Super Admin, Admin or OB.

Build in this order. Each step needs the one before it:

1. Course category
2. Course (with modules and lessons)
3. Question group and questions (for the quiz)
4. Quiz, assignment and assessment
5. Quiz setup
6. Check what students see

### Add modules and lessons

Open the course (Course Management → the course → **Modules**), then **Add module**. Inside each module, **Add lesson**. Build the whole sample curriculum below: 4 modules, 12 lessons, about 12 hours.

**Module fields:** Module name *, Status (Published to show it to students, Draft to hide it), Description.
**Lesson fields:** Lesson name *, Duration (minutes) *, Host, the content for that host, Privacy, Description.

Choose the **Host** per lesson. The content field changes with it:

| Host | What to enter |
|---|---|
| Editor | Type or paste the lesson text (use the sample text below) |
| YouTube | A youtube.com or youtu.be link |
| Video URL | A direct link to an MP4 or WebM file |
| Google Drive | A share link ("Anyone with the link") |
| PPT file, PDF file, Word file, Text file, Images | Upload the file |

The lessons below use **Editor** so you can paste the text exactly; swap any of them for a video link or an uploaded PDF or presentation.

#### Module 1: Introductions and first impressions
Status: Published. Description: How to greet, introduce yourself and make a good first impression.

| # | Lesson name | Minutes | Host |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introducing yourself | 20 | Editor |
| 2 | Body language and eye contact | 25 | YouTube |
| 3 | Small talk that builds trust | 20 | Editor |

**Lesson 1 text (Editor):**
> **Introducing yourself.** Say your name clearly, say what you do or study, and finish with one thing you enjoy. Keep it under thirty seconds.
> Example: "Hello, I am Priya. I study commerce at Fatima College. I enjoy reading and teaching younger students."
> Practise: write your own introduction in three sentences and say it aloud twice.

**Lesson 2:** paste a YouTube link about body language in interviews. Description: Stand tall, keep an open posture, look at the listener, smile naturally.

**Lesson 3 text (Editor):**
> **Small talk.** Start with something shared: the weather, the journey, the venue. Ask an open question ("How did you find the session?") and listen to the answer. Avoid money, religion, politics and personal complaints.

---

#### Module 2: Professional writing
Status: Published. Description: Emails, messages and short documents that get a reply.

| # | Lesson name | Minutes | Host |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Writing a professional email | 30 | Editor |
| 2 | Email do's and don'ts | 20 | PDF file |
| 3 | Writing a short message or report | 25 | Word file |

**Lesson 1 text (Editor):**
> **A clear email has six parts:** a subject line that says the point, a polite greeting, one sentence of purpose, the details, a thank-you, and your name.
> Example subject: "Request to move Thursday session to Friday".
> Example body: "Dear Sir, I would like to request moving Thursday's session to Friday because of a college exam. Please let me know if that works. Thank you. Priya R."

**Lesson 2:** upload a one-page PDF titled "Email do's and don'ts". Suggested content: Do use a clear subject, proofread, reply within a day. Don't use slang, capital letters for emphasis, or "reply all" without a reason.

**Lesson 3:** upload a Word template with headings Purpose, Findings and Next steps, with a few lines under each.

---

#### Module 3: Speaking and presenting
Status: Published. Description: Plan and deliver a short presentation with confidence.

| # | Lesson name | Minutes | Host |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planning a two-minute talk | 25 | Editor |
| 2 | Designing simple slides | 30 | PPT file |
| 3 | Handling nerves and questions | 20 | YouTube |

**Lesson 1 text (Editor):**
> **Plan in three parts.** Opening (who you are and what you will say), middle (two or three points, one example each), closing (one sentence to remember). Time it. Two minutes is about 250 words.

**Lesson 2:** upload a short PowerPoint (5 slides) showing a title slide, one idea per slide, large text and a closing slide. Description: no more than six lines per slide, one image, high contrast.

**Lesson 3:** paste a YouTube link about managing speaking anxiety. Description: breathe slowly, pause before answering, say "Let me think about that" if you need a moment.

---

#### Module 4: Teamwork and workplace habits
Status: Published. Description: Working with others, meeting deadlines and receiving feedback.

| # | Lesson name | Minutes | Host |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Working in a team | 25 | Editor |
| 2 | Time management and deadlines | 25 | Google Drive |
| 3 | Giving and receiving feedback | 20 | Editor |

**Lesson 1 text (Editor):**
> **Good teammates** share the plan, say what they will do, finish what they promise, and tell the team early if they are stuck. Disagree with the idea, not the person.

**Lesson 2:** a Google Drive share link to a weekly planner sheet. Description: list tasks, mark urgent and important, schedule the hard task first.

**Lesson 3 text (Editor):**
> **Receiving feedback:** listen without interrupting, thank the person, ask one question to understand, then decide what to change. **Giving feedback:** be specific, kind and about the work ("The opening was clear; add an example in the middle").

#### Checking the curriculum

- Open the course → **Modules**: you should see 4 modules, each with 3 lessons.
- Keep every module **Published**; a student sees only published modules, and a mentor can unlock a locked one early from **Unlock Module**.
- Lessons add up to 285 minutes of content; the course is set to 12 hours so students also have time for practice and the assessments.

## 3. Question group and questions (the quiz's content)

**Assessment Library → Quiz tab → Question groups**, then **Question bank**.

First create a group (a set of questions) under **Question groups**: **Title \*** = `Module 1 quiz questions`. (You can also pick "Add a custom group…" inside the question form.)

Then, under **Question bank**, **Add question**. Choose the group, then a question type.

| Question type | What to fill in |
|---|---|
| Multiple choice | Question, marks, the options, tick the correct answer(s) |
| Sorting | Question and the items in the correct order |
| Matching | Question and the matching pairs |
| Puzzle | Question and the answer word or phrase, plus an optional hint |
| Short answer | Question, accepted keywords |
| Long answer | Question, marking guide or model answer, optional word limit (a mentor marks these by hand) |

Sample questions:

**Multiple choice**, 2 marks
- Question: Which is the best way to start an introduction?
- Options: "Hello, I am Priya." (correct) / "Um, so…" / "You know me." / "Nothing to say."
- Explanation: A clear greeting and your name set a confident tone.

**Multiple choice**, 2 marks (more than one correct)
- Question: Which belong in a professional email? Tick all that apply.
- Options: A clear subject line (correct) / A polite greeting (correct) / Slang and emojis / Your signature (correct)

**Sorting**, 3 marks
- Question: Put the parts of a short introduction in order.
- Items in order: Greeting, Name, What you do, Something you enjoy

**Matching**, 3 marks
- Question: Match each word to its use.
- Pairs: Subject line = tells the reader what the email is about; Greeting = opens politely; Signature = closes with your name

**Short answer**, 2 marks
- Question: Name one thing to avoid in a professional email.
- Accepted keywords: slang, emoji, all caps, rude

**Long answer**, 5 marks
- Question: Write a short email asking your mentor to move a session to Friday.
- Marking guide: Clear subject, polite greeting, reason, requested change, thanks, name. 80 to 120 words.
- Word limit: 150

## 4. Quiz, assignment and assessment

**Assessment Library**, then the **Quiz**, **Assignment** or **Assessment** tab (Quiz → **Quizzes** list), and the create button. One form handles all three. The **Type** is set from the tab; the fields change with it.

### Quiz

| Field | Sample value |
|---|---|
| Type | Quiz |
| Title * | Module 1 Quiz: Introductions |
| Course * | Professional Communication Skills (the course title, exactly) |
| Module | Module 1: Introductions and first impressions |
| Topic / lesson | Introducing yourself |
| Status | Published (Draft to prepare, Closed to stop new attempts) |
| Question groups | Module 1 quiz questions |

A quiz has no submit date or description. Its total marks are the sum of its groups' question marks (here 2 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 5 = 17).

### Assignment

| Field | Sample value |
|---|---|
| Type | Assignment |
| Title * | Assignment 1: Record your introduction |
| Course * | Professional Communication Skills |
| Module | Module 1: Introductions and first impressions |
| Topic / lesson | Introducing yourself |
| Status | Published |
| Submit date * | a date in the future (the deadline; no submissions after it) |
| Marks | 20 |
| Minimum percentage | 50 |
| Assignment document | Optional brief the student downloads: Word, PowerPoint, PDF or an image, shown in a viewer |
| Description | Write a 100-word introduction about yourself and attach it as a document. Cover your name, studies, strengths and goal. |

Students upload their work as one or more attachments. A student can withdraw a submission before a mentor opens it. A mentor or admin reviews it, then grades it with marks (not above the maximum) and feedback, or returns it for changes.

| Setting | Sample value |
|---|---|

| Pass percentage | 60 |
| Multiple attempts | On, with Attempts allowed = 2 |

Long-answer questions need a mentor to mark them, so the student sees "some answers need a mentor to mark them" until then.

## 6. Check what students see

1. The course must be **Published**, mapped to the student's batch.
2. Quiz, assignment and assessment must be **Published** and use the course's exact title in **Course**.
3. Sign in as a student in that batch:
   - **My Learning** lists the course, its modules and lessons.
   - **Assessments** lists the quiz, assignment and assessment. The assignment and assessment open with the description and attachment; the quiz opens an attempt with the timer.
4. After the student submits, the bell shows notifications on both sides: staff get "submitted", the student gets the result or "graded".
